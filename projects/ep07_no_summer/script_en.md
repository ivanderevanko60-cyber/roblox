# The Year Summer Never Came

Target: ~5 min narration. Lines kept short: one idea each, so the narration can breathe and the cut can move.
Format per block: **VO** → *VISUAL*.

---

## 0. Cold open (0:00–0:45)

**VO(excited):** In June 1816, it snowed in New England.
*VISUAL: snow falling on green leaves, a farm — fast*

**VO(confident):** Not a light frost. Snow — in the first week of summer — on fields that had just been planted.
*VISUAL: frozen crops, frost on plants*

**VO(excited):** In Europe, it rained for months. The harvest rotted. People ate moss, nettles and cats.
*VISUAL: storm clouds, rain, empty fields — fast*

**VO(sad):** And in a villa on Lake Geneva, an eighteen-year-old stuck indoors by the storms started writing Frankenstein.
*VISUAL: candle, pen, dark window with rain*

**VO:** Nobody alive knew why. The cause was on the other side of the world — and it had happened a year earlier.
*VISUAL: globe turning, ocean*

**VO(confident):** This is the story of the volcano that cancelled summer for the whole planet.
*VISUAL: volcano erupting; title over footage*

---

## I. The loudest sound (0:45–1:45)

**VO:** In April 1815, on the Indonesian island of Sumbawa, a mountain called Tambora woke up.
*VISUAL: tropical island, volcano*

**VO(excited):** The explosions were heard more than two thousand kilometres away. Soldiers on other islands thought it was cannon fire, and went looking for pirates.
*VISUAL: sea, ships, smoke*

**VO:** It was the biggest eruption in recorded history. The mountain lost more than a kilometre of its height.
*VISUAL: lava, ash column*

**VO(sad):** Around ten thousand people died in the eruption itself. Tens of thousands more died in the famine that followed on the islands nearby.
*VISUAL: ash, grey landscape*

**VO:** But the ash that fell on Sumbawa wasn't the dangerous part.
*VISUAL: ash falling*

**VO(confident):** The dangerous part went up.
*VISUAL: smoke rising into the sky, slow*

---

## II. A veil around the Earth (1:45–2:35)

**VO:** Tambora blew around sixty million tonnes of sulphur gas into the stratosphere — twenty, thirty, forty kilometres up.
*VISUAL: plume from above, clouds from a plane*

**VO:** Up there, there's no rain to wash it out. The gas turned into a haze of tiny droplets, and the winds spread it around the entire planet.
*VISUAL: high clouds, Earth from orbit*

**VO(confident):** A veil, too thin to see — but thick enough to bounce sunlight back into space.
*VISUAL: sun through haze*

**VO:** The whole Northern Hemisphere cooled by around half a degree. That sounds like nothing.
*VISUAL: thermometer, cold morning*

**VO(sad):** For farmers, it was everything.
*VISUAL: farmer, field, hands in soil*

---

## III. Eighteen hundred and froze to death (2:35–3:40)

**VO:** In New England, there was frost in every month of 1816. Corn froze in the fields in June, again in July, again in August.
*VISUAL: frost, corn, grey sky*

**VO(cheerful):** Americans gave the year a nickname: eighteen hundred and froze to death.
*VISUAL: snowy farmhouse*

**VO:** Thousands of families gave up on New England altogether and moved west, to Ohio and beyond.
*VISUAL: wagons, road, open land*

**VO(sad):** In Europe it was worse. The price of wheat doubled. There were food riots in Britain and France. In Switzerland, people starved.
*VISUAL: bread, market, crowds, rain*

**VO:** In China's Yunnan province, the rice harvest failed. In India, the monsoon went wrong.
*VISUAL: rice terraces, rain over fields*

**VO(cheerful):** Even horses went hungry — there were no oats. Some historians think that's why, the next year, a German inventor built a horse you didn't have to feed: the first bicycle.
*VISUAL: horse, old bicycle, wheel*

---

## IV. Two monsters (3:40–4:10)

**VO:** Back on Lake Geneva, the storms kept a group of young English writers indoors for days.
*VISUAL: lake, storm, villa windows*

**VO(cheerful):** Lord Byron, bored, challenged everyone to write a ghost story.
*VISUAL: candles, fire, glasses*

**VO:** Mary Shelley came up with Frankenstein. Byron's doctor, John Polidori, wrote The Vampyre — the first modern vampire story.
*VISUAL: pen, pages, dark corridor*

**VO:** Byron himself wrote a poem called Darkness — about the day the Sun goes out.
*VISUAL: grey sun, dim sky*

**VO(confident):** One cold summer gave us two of the most famous monsters in fiction.
*VISUAL: lightning, storm*

---

## V. The sky in the paintings (4:10–4:40)

**VO:** And everywhere the veil went, the sunsets turned strange.
*VISUAL: sunset, deep red sky*

**VO:** For years afterwards, the skies over Europe glowed blood red and orange.
*VISUAL: orange sky timelapse*

**VO(confident):** Scientists have measured the colours in paintings from those years. After big eruptions, the painted sunsets really do get redder.
*VISUAL: paint, brush, canvas, gallery*

---

## VI. Nobody knew (4:40–5:10)

**VO:** At the time, nobody connected any of this to a volcano on the other side of the world.
*VISUAL: old map, candlelight*

**VO:** It took more than a century — until an American physicist named William Humphreys put the numbers together in 1920.
*VISUAL: old books, notes*

**VO(confident):** In 1991, we got to watch it happen again. Mount Pinatubo in the Philippines cooled the whole planet by about half a degree for two years.
*VISUAL: eruption, ash cloud*

---

## VII. The last digit (5:10–5:40)

**VO:** Tambora threw out more than ten times as much rock as Pinatubo.
*VISUAL: volcano, slow*

**VO:** Volcanologists put the chance of another eruption that size this century at about one in six.
*VISUAL: dice, a roll*

**VO(confident):** One in six. The same odds as rolling a die.
*VISUAL: a die landing*

**VO:** This time, at least, we'd know why the summer didn't come.
*VISUAL: sunset, end card*
